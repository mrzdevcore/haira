% Preface
\chapter*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Preface}

\haira{} is a statically-typed, compiled programming language for building AI agents and workflows. It combines the simplicity of Go, the safety of Rust, and the expressiveness of modern functional languages, while making agents, tools, and workflows first-class language constructs---not library imports.

\section*{Design Philosophy}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Agents and workflows are primitives} -- \keyword{provider}, \keyword{tool}, \keyword{agent}, and \keyword{workflow} are language keywords, not framework abstractions.
    \item \textbf{Type-safe orchestration} -- Agent inputs, outputs, and tool schemas are verified at compile time.
    \item \textbf{Go-style simplicity} -- Familiar error handling, explicit imports, and straightforward syntax.
    \item \textbf{Zero boilerplate} -- No framework setup, no dependency injection, no configuration files.
    \item \textbf{Native performance} -- Compiles to Go, then to standalone native binaries via \code{go build}.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Target Domains}

\haira{} replaces the entire agentic stack:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Python + LangChain/LangGraph} -- for code-first agent builders
    \item \textbf{n8n / Make / Zapier} -- for workflow automation
    \item \textbf{CrewAI / AutoGen} -- for multi-agent systems
    \item \textbf{Custom chatbot backends} -- REST and WebSocket with streaming
    \item \textbf{General-purpose programming} -- CLI tools, web services, data pipelines
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Reserved Keywords}
\label{app:keywords}

The following identifiers are reserved as keywords and cannot be used as identifiers:

\begin{lstlisting}[numbers=none]
agent     and       as        assert    break
catch     chan      const     continue  defer
else      enum      errdefer  eval      export
false     fn        for       from      if
impl      import    in        let       match
nil       not       or        provider  pub
return    select    some      spawn     step
struct    test      tool      trait     true
try       type      unsafe    verify    where
while     workflow
\end{lstlisting}

\chapter{Operator Precedence}
\label{app:precedence}

Operators are listed from highest to lowest precedence:

\begin{longtable}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Precedence} & \textbf{Operators} & \textbf{Associativity} \\
\midrule
\endhead
1 (highest) & \code{()} \code{[]} \code{.} & Left \\
2 & \code{!} \code{-} \code{\~{}} (unary) \code{not} & Right \\
3 & \code{*} \code{/} \code{\%} & Left \\
4 & \code{+} \code{-} & Left \\
5 & \code{<<} \code{>>} & Left \\
6 & \code{\&} (bitwise AND) & Left \\
7 & \code{\^{}} (bitwise XOR) & Left \\
8 & \code{|} (bitwise OR) & Left \\
9 & \code{..} \code{..=} & None \\
10 & \code{|>} (pipe) & Left \\
11 & \code{<} \code{>} \code{<=} \code{>=} & Left \\
12 & \code{==} \code{!=} & Left \\
13 & \code{and} \code{\&\&} & Left \\
14 & \code{or} \code{||} & Left \\
15 (lowest) & \code{=} \code{+=} \code{-=} \code{*=} \code{/=} \code{\&=} \code{|=} \code{\^{}=} \code{<<=} \code{>>=} & Right \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
